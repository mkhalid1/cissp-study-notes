# For hands-on practitioners: thinking like a manager

If you are a working security or IT professional, such as an engineer, analyst, administrator or consultant, your experience is what ISC2 requires. It also explains why strong technical people miss items. The exam asks what a senior security professional should do for the organisation, and that is often not what a skilled engineer would do at the keyboard.

## Why technical instincts cost points

At work, when something is broken, you fix it. On the CISSP, the right answer is usually to understand the risk, follow the process and involve the right owner first. The exam is written from the point of view of someone accountable to the business: a security manager, an adviser to senior management, a programme owner.

Signs your technical instinct is leading you astray:

- You are choosing an option because you know how to configure it.
- You are picturing commands, ports or vendor products.
- The option you like fixes the symptom quickly but skips approval, documentation or the owner.
- You are about to pick the most technically sophisticated control in the list.

## The manager's order of thinking

When several options are true, these priorities usually decide which is BEST:

1. **People's safety** before anything else.
2. **Understand the business** and its goals.
3. **Assess the risk** before choosing controls.
4. **Get management direction and support,** because they own the risk and the budget.
5. **Policy and process** before tools.
6. **Fix the root cause,** not only the symptom.
7. **Prevent** where you can, **detect** where you cannot, **correct and recover** when both fail.
8. **Advise, escalate and document.** Let the owner decide; do not make their decision for them.

## Worked comparison

| Situation | What a busy engineer often does | What the CISSP usually rewards | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A critical vulnerability is found on a production server | Patch it right away | Assess the risk, then patch through the emergency change process | Untested changes cause outages; change control protects availability and accountability |
| You discover an employee may be stealing data | Lock their account and confront them | Report to management and HR, preserve evidence, involve legal | Investigations have legal rules; tipping off a suspect destroys evidence |
| A new cloud service is proposed | Start configuring security groups | Understand business requirements and data sensitivity, then assess the provider | Controls follow requirements and risk |
| Malware is spreading | Start cleaning machines | Contain first: isolate affected systems | Cleaning before containing lets it spread and loses evidence |
| Management wants to skip a control to hit a deadline | Refuse, or quietly comply | Explain the risk and let management formally accept or reject it | Management owns risk decisions; your job is to make the risk clear |
| You find a flaw in a vendor's product | Post a warning online | Report privately to the vendor first | Coordinated disclosure protects everyone who uses it |

## When the technical answer is right

Sometimes the technical option is the right one. When the stem already establishes the governance (the risk is assessed, management has approved, the change is authorised) and asks which control works, choose the control that works best. Read for the stage: if the decision has been made, the question is about doing it well.

## Experience, the Associate path and endorsement

To become certified, ISC2 requires:

- **Experience:** a minimum of five years cumulative, full-time experience in two or more of the eight domains of the current outline. Part-time work and internships may also count.
- **Waiver:** a bachelor's or master's degree in computer science, IT or a related field, **or** an additional credential from ISC2's approved list, may satisfy up to one year. Only one year can be waived in total.
- **Associate of ISC2:** if you pass without enough experience, you become an Associate of ISC2 and have six years to earn the five years required.
- **Endorsement:** after passing, you must complete the certification application within **nine months** of your exam date. An ISC2 certified professional in good standing endorses your experience. If you do not know one, you can ask ISC2 to endorse you, which requires proof of employment.
- **Keeping it:** CISSP holders earn **120 CPE credits** over each three-year cycle and pay an annual maintenance fee. ISC2 publishes a suggested annual minimum to spread the work, but there is no annual requirement unless you hold Associate status.

Check each rule on ISC2's own pages before you rely on it; they are listed in [Sources](sources.md).

## Practise the mindset

The fastest way to build the habit is volume: many scenario items, each followed by asking why the chosen answer beats the runner-up. Every domain guide in these notes explains why the nearest wrong option loses.
