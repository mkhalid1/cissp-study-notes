# Timing and pacing

The CISSP gives you a maximum of 3 hours for 100 to 150 items, with no going back. Good pacing means reaching item 150, if you get that far, with a clear head, and never letting one item steal time from ten others.

## The budget

| Figure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum time | 3 hours (180 minutes) | ISC2 |
| Items | 100 minimum, 150 maximum | ISC2 |
| Average per item if you get 150 | 72 seconds | Arithmetic |
| Average per item if you get 100 | 108 seconds | Arithmetic |
| Breaks | Not limited in number or length, but all break time counts toward the 3 hours | ISC2 |
| Minimum you must answer | 75 scored plus 25 pretest items, or you fail automatically | ISC2 |

Plan for the worst case: 150 items. At 72 seconds each, you finish with no spare time, so aim a little faster.

## A pacing plan

This is our study guidance, not an ISC2 rule. Aim for about **65 seconds per item**, which leaves roughly 15 minutes for a short break and for the occasional slow item.

| Checkpoint | You should be at about |
|---|---|
| 30 minutes | Item 27 |
| 60 minutes | Item 55 |
| 90 minutes | Item 83 |
| 120 minutes | Item 110 |
| 150 minutes | Item 138 |
| 165 minutes | Item 150 |

Most testing screens show the time remaining and the item number. Glance at them at the checkpoints, not after every item.

## How to handle a hard item

1. **Read the last line of the stem first** so you know what is being asked: FIRST, BEST, MOST, or a fact.
2. **Read the stem fully once,** looking for the decisive words: role, stage, asset, constraint.
3. **Eliminate two options.** Most items have two that are clearly off.
4. **Choose between the last two** with the manager's lens: risk, people, policy, root cause.
5. **If you pass 90 seconds,** commit to the better of the two and move on. You cannot come back, so spending three minutes on one item only steals time from later ones.

## Where time usually goes

- **Rereading the stem because the options look alike.** Decide what you think the answer is before reading the options.
- **Technical rabbit holes.** Practitioners can spend minutes picturing how they would configure something. The question rarely needs that level.
- **Second-guessing after answering.** You cannot change it. Let it go and give the next item your full attention.
- **Long breaks.** A short break can help, but the clock keeps running.

## Breaks

ISC2 does not limit the number or length of breaks, but every minute counts toward your 3 hours. As guidance, if you take one, take it around the 90-minute mark, keep it to five or ten minutes, and use it to reset rather than to replay questions.

## Practise against the clock

Do practice sets in one sitting, with a timer, and **without going back** to earlier items, so the real exam feels familiar. The [free CISSP practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/cissp?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=cissp) is a timed set you can use for this.
